# Fundamental Theorem

## First Statement

> If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, and $F(x) = \int_{a}^{x} f(t) dt$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and differentiable on $(a, b)$, then the derivative of $F$ is $f(x)$.

Or in simpler terms, derivative cancels out integral and the upper limit is substituted inside the inner function.

> [!note] Upper limit is a function?
> If upper limit itself is a function or a non-variable like $x^3 + 1$, then chain rule must be applied.


## Second Statement

> If $f$ is continuous over $[a, b]$ and $F$ is an anti-derivative of $f$ on $[a,b]$, then: $$ \int_{a}^{b} f(x) dx = F(b) - F(a)$$
> This is the Evaluation Theorem.
