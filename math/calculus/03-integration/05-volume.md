# Volume

Integrals can also be used to calculate the volume of a solid using it's cross section area.

$$V = \int_{a}^{b} A(x) dx$$

There are three main methods to find volume.

## Disc Method

> Infinitely thin cylinders perperndicular to the axis of rotation are added up to get the volume.

Use When -> Slices taken perpendicular to axis of rotation.

$$V = \int_{a}^{b} \pi R(x)^{2} dx$$

## Washer Method

> The volume of the inner empty cylinders (infinitely many from the above method) is subtracted from the volume of the outer filled cylinders.

Use When -> Slices taken perpendicular to the axis of rotation have a hole in them.

$$V = \int_{a}^{b} \pi [R(x)^{2} - r(x)^{2}] dx$$

## Cylindrical Shell Method

> The volume of nested cylindrical shells parallel to the axis of rotation are added up.

Use When -> Slicing parallel to the axis of rotation. Helps when the axis of rotation is along the y-axis.

$$V = \int_{a}^{b} 2\pi (shell \space radius) (shell \space height) dx$$
